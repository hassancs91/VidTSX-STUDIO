# Talking-head short

A short lives or dies in its first two seconds and never gives the viewer a
reason to leave. Everything below serves retention.

## The hook rule

- The first spoken line on the timeline must be the strongest claim, question
  or result in the take — not a greeting, not "in this video". If the speaker
  opens with a greeting, the editorial pass cuts it and the video starts on
  the hook line. Say which line you chose as the hook and why.
- No intro card, no logo sting. A word-synced title over the hook line is the
  only opening shot allowed, and it must not delay the first word.
- If the take restates the hook later, keep the later restatement only when it
  adds a number or a consequence; otherwise cut it as fluff.

## Pacing

- Auto Cut runs aggressive: every pause longer than a breath goes.
- The editorial pass is content-aggressive: cut every retake, false start,
  filler and any sentence that explains what is about to be explained.
- Target one visual change every 12–15 seconds: a cutaway, an overlay, a
  b-roll clip or a caption style change counts. Never let 20 seconds pass
  with the same frame.
- No crossfades or dips. Hard cuts only; a transition reads as slow.

## Shots

- Cutaways are full-frame, high-contrast, one idea per shot: a number, a
  three-word claim, a product close-up. Text is large enough to read on a
  phone held at arm's length — one to five words per screen.
- Overlays sit in the upper third so captions keep the lower third.
- Every shot is anchored to the words it illustrates; unanchored shots are
  reserved for the outro.

## Captions

- Always on, word-synced, two or three words per group, the emphasised word
  in the accent colour. Uppercase is fine for a short.
- Captions never cover the speaker's mouth: keep them in the lower third of a
  9:16 frame.

## Sound

- One music bed for the whole short, ducked under speech, cut hard on the
  last word.
- A whoosh or hit on every cutaway entrance and on the hook line. If sound
  tools are unavailable, say so in one line and keep going.

## Outro

- Two seconds at most: the last spoken line plus one overlay with the call to
  action. No end card that outlives the audio.
