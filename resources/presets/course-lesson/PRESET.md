# Course lesson

A lesson is watched to be understood, often twice. Nothing is cut for pace
that costs clarity, and every hard idea gets a picture.

## Chapter structure

- Open with a chapter title card: the lesson name and, underneath, the two or
  three things the viewer will be able to do afterwards. Anchor it to the
  speaker's first sentence; it may run up to ten seconds.
- Every lesson has a "what you will learn" beat, a body of two to five
  concepts, and a recap. Find them in the transcript; if the recap is
  missing, say so — do not invent one.
- Each concept opens with a small persistent overlay in a corner carrying the
  concept name, so a viewer who scrubs back can see where they are.

## Pacing

- Auto Cut runs light: remove only dead air longer than two seconds. Pauses
  are where learning happens.
- The editorial pass cuts retakes and false starts only. Fillers stay unless
  they sit between two takes; "fluff" is almost always a re-explanation, and
  re-explanations are the point of a lesson — keep them, suggest nothing.
- A dip-to-black (0.6 s) separates concepts. Hard cuts inside a concept.

## Shots

- Diagrams over the hard parts: when the speaker describes a process, a
  relationship or a comparison, propose a cutaway that draws it — boxes and
  arrows, a table, a before/after. Plain, high-contrast, no motion beyond a
  build-in.
- Code, formulas and definitions become full-frame cutaways that stay on
  screen for as long as the speaker discusses them.
- Two shots a minute is a ceiling, not a target. A minute of clear
  explanation needs none.

## Captions

- On, two rolling lines in the lower third, sentence case, punctuated. They
  are read, not felt — no emphasis colouring, no bounce.

## Sound

- No music bed. No sound effects. Silence is fine in a lesson.

## Recap and outro

- The recap gets one overlay listing the concepts as they are named. Close
  on the speaker's last line and a six-second end card that repeats the
  lesson name and the next lesson's, if the script names it.
